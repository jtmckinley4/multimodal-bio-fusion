## What changed after the presentation

### The dataset targets are now connected to their measurements

The method guides and notebook explanations now trace the biological assay through the target transformation to the quantity our predictors are scored against. This changes the interpretation of the results, not the saved labels or experiment. The detailed examples and source papers remain in [Data and targets](../methods/data-and-targets.md).

| Dataset or task | What the supplied target means | Consequence for interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mRFP synonymous variants | Natural log of red fluorescence corrected using the GFPuv green reference. The source-to-CSV calculation was checked. | A score describes prediction of log corrected fluorescence, not a measured change in protein-production rate. Equal fold changes correspond to equal log differences. |
| BioLangFusion mRNA stability | Sign-harmonized decay-profile slopes standardized within source profiles. | This is a pooled relative stability target, not half-life in hours; source profiles can represent different biological settings. |
| CoV-Vac degradation | Mean degradation measurement over 68 positions, then standardized across examples. | Chemical RNA degradation is distinct from intracellular RNA turnover. Translating a cropped fragment into a computational protein input does not establish a biological reading frame. |
| Fungal expression | The supplied values match the upstream `lnExprs` field. | Some upstream units, normalization details and organism mapping remain unresolved; a log-expression label alone does not settle them. |
| E. coli production | Original gel-based expression scores collapsed into three production classes. | Accuracy measures recovery of those categories, not protein concentration or solubility directly. |
| GTEx pilot | A vector of 30 tissue-averaged transcript-expression targets, supplied after a `log(x + 1)` transform of TPM. | The local release leaves the logarithm base unresolved. Tissue averaging and normalization matter when comparing with the paper. |
| Human full-transcript half-life and TE | Half-life uses a standardized summary of source decay measurements; TE uses an RNA-abundance-adjusted ribosome-occupancy measure. | Neither target is simply protein output per RNA molecule. A longer-lived RNA can be translated more often without increasing its translation rate. |

For example, GFPuv normalization assumes some shared experimental variation affects red and green signals proportionally; it does not correct every biological difference. Folding calculations in the mRFP controls are sequence-derived structural or energy summaries, not measured intracellular RNA structures. These distinctions now connect the biology to the numerical comparisons rather than relying on the dataset names. [mRFP explanation](../../Code/stage4/README.md#mrfp-changing-the-nucleotide-sequence-while-keeping-the-protein-fixed) · [Full-transcript explanation](../../Code/stage4/README.md#full-transcript-half-life-and-translation-efficiency).

The intervals use 2,000 paired bootstrap draws, keeping repeated exact sequences together. The requested three-seed summaries are withheld because seeds 1 and 2 are absent. Biological interpretation should explain what information could be useful without claiming that the scores identify a mechanism. In CoV-Vac, a computational translation may offer another representation of nucleotide patterns without supplying evidence of protein biology. In coding-sequence tasks, an amino-acid representation discards synonymous-codon distinctions while emphasizing patterns learned from proteins; a nucleotide representation can retain those distinctions. That is a reason to test complementarity, not proof that a particular gain is caused by codon regulation. Cross-attention also changes projections, residual connections and normalization, so its configuration-level benefit cannot be assigned solely to attention weights. ## Literature and dataset direction

The October 29 notes emphasized choosing a task and identifying relevant information before selecting a fusion architecture. They also raised codon-level gating, encoder layer selection, RNA structure, Coupled Mamba and conditional IsoFormer fine-tuning. These remain attributed discussion ideas, not evidence that an architecture is biologically explanatory or that every suggestion should become an experiment. [Meeting notes](Mina_Meeting_Notes.md#october-29-2026-follow-up-on-the-stage-4-round-1-deck).

Chase's reading of *Fusion or Confusion?* foregrounds strong unimodal and simple-fusion controls with matched evaluation. Its SimBaMM combines projected encoder representations with a Transformer head, so our concatenation head is not SimBaMM. The paper studies fusion after separate encoders; “late” does not mean only averaging predictions. It motivates a controlled Coupled Mamba comparison without establishing a biological-task advantage. [Fusion or Confusion?, Sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22991v4).

The working paper direction is therefore to develop task-specific expectations from the input information and encoder evidence, then test those expectations against strong constituent and simple-fusion controls. A deeper mechanistic biological study could be a later contribution. Neither novelty nor publishability is established merely by observing that fusion sometimes helps. Chase reports asking Julian to develop a Coupled Mamba notebook; this account does not claim that implementation or its results have been verified.
